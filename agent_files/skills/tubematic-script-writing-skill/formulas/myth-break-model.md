# Myth, Break, Model

`formula_id`: `myth-break-model`

State the belief the viewer holds in its strongest form, break it in one line, then build the right model on a case the viewer is living through right now.

## Length

120 to 240 words across every segment's `voice`. Stay inside the band: the beats below are sized for it.

## Beats

In this order. A share is the part of the script's words the beat covers; hold each within a few points.

| Beat | Share of the script | Its job | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| The belief | 0 to 8% | As the viewer learned it, or as a warning in the viewer's own words. | - |
| The break | 8 to 12% | One line: that is wrong, or that stopped being true. | the turn: a visible change on its first word |
| Case A | 12 to 35% | Where the old belief seems to hold, and what is really happening there. | - |
| Case B | 35 to 60% | Where it fails. One real named source, study or number. | a re-opening: a visible change on its first word |
| Tie to the viewer | 60 to 75% | The thing the viewer is doing right now is case B. Or the viewer's objection, spoken. | a re-opening: a visible change on its first word |
| The precise truth | 75 to 90% | One sentence. | the payoff: the video's strongest picture |
| Second sting or action | 90 to 100% | A related well-known 'fact' that is also wrong, or the one thing to do. | - |

A segment's `visual` brief for a beat with an on-screen duty names the change it asks for. The scene author reads the same duty from the timeline.

## First frame

The belief as the viewer remembers it, drawn the familiar way, so it can be struck out on the word that breaks it. Segment 1's `visual` brief asks for this picture, already moving, and it is the subject of the first sentence.

## Openings

Draft one opening from each of three shapes. The type goes in `hooks[].type`.

| Type (`type`) | Shape |
|---|---|
| Belief, then break (`myth`) | You were taught [belief]. That is wrong. |
| Flat claim (`claim`) | [Thing everybody does to stay safe or healthy] does nothing for [what it is meant to protect]. |
| Question (`question`) | If [belief] were true, why does [everyday counter-example] happen? |

## What holds the viewer

The belief is the viewer's own, so the answer has to overturn something they hold. A pair of contrasting cases carries the middle. The viewer's objection, spoken at about three fifths, re-opens the question.

## Ending

The precise statement, then a second sting or one concrete action. No moral.

## Pitfalls

- Confirming the myth at length before breaking it
- A hedge where the verdict belongs
- An invented study: the source must be real and named

## Template

The shape of the whole script with the facts left out. Fill it with this topic's facts; never keep its wording.

> You were taught [belief]. That is wrong. When [case A], [what really happens]. But [case B] breaks it: [real named fact or study]. [The thing you are doing right now] is [case B]. So the truth is [precise statement]. And [a related well-known fact] is wrong too.
