# Escalating Ladder

`formula_id`: `escalating-ladder`

Name a measure and both ends, then climb: each step is a label, a number and a picture the viewer knows, joined to the last by a comparison, with the strangest step last.

## Length

110 to 420 words across every segment's `voice`. Stay inside the band: the beats below are sized for it.

## Beats

In this order. A share is the part of the script's words the beat covers; hold each within a few points.

| Beat | Share of the script | Its job |
|---|---|---|
| Frame | 0 to 12% | The measure and both ends, or the bottom step tied to something in the viewer's world. |
| Steps 1 to 3 | 12 to 55% | One step at a time: a label, one number, one picture. Each transition compares it with the step before. |
| Leave the frame | 55 to 70% | Go past where the viewer thought it ended, or speak their objection. For a what-if: the law that should stop it, then set it aside. |
| Top step | 70 to 92% | The thing the title named, or the most extreme case. |
| Twist or stop | 92 to 100% | Stop on the number, or redefine what 'top' means. |

## Openings

Draft one opening from each of three shapes. The type goes in `hooks[].type`.

| Type (`type`) | Shape |
|---|---|
| Both ends named (`frame`) | From [the cheap end] all the way to [the expensive end]. The test: [one thing you can measure]. |
| Number (`number`) | In [year], [the old thing] held [a small amount], about [one familiar object]. |
| What if (`what_if`) | Here is what would happen if you [premise]. |
| Question (`question`) | How many [X] can you [lose] and still [function]? Start with the easy ones. |

## What holds the viewer

Escalation is the engine: every transition is a comparison with the step before. A running score is a progress bar. The strongest re-opening comes where the ladder leaves the frame the viewer expected. A long what-if ladder earns its length only by re-opening the question every few steps.

## Ending

The top step's number, or a twist on what the top is. Stop there.

## Pitfalls

- The surprise spent in the first sentence
- Steps of equal weight
- A list cut into parts
- Numbers with nothing to compare them to

## Template

The shape of the whole script with the facts left out. Fill it with this topic's facts; never keep its wording.

> [The measure] runs from [bottom] to [top]. Bottom: [item 1], [number], about [a picture the viewer knows]. Next: [item 2], [comparison], [number], [a bigger picture]. Next: [item 3]. You might think that is the top. [The step beyond]. Top: [the title's item], [its most vivid number]. The real top: [twist].
