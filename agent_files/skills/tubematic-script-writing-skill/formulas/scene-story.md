# Scene Story

`formula_id`: `scene-story`

Put a person, named only by role, inside an act already under way. Turn it with 'but when' inside the first fifth, hold the reveal to the last quarter, and stop on a hard fact.

## Length

90 to 200 words across every segment's `voice`. Stay inside the band: the beats below are sized for it.

## Beats

In this order. A share is the part of the script's words the beat covers; hold each within a few points.

| Beat | Share of the script | Its job |
|---|---|---|
| The act | 0 to 15% | A pronoun or a role, in the middle of doing something. No name and no date with a life history. |
| But when | 15 to 30% | The first change of direction. |
| Escalation | 30 to 55% | Remove the ways out. Plant one detail for later. |
| False relief | 55 to 65% | It seems to be over. |
| Reveal | 65 to 85% | As a plain noun or a look back: bigger than the threat the viewer knew about. |
| Last line | 85 to 100% | A hard fact, an ironic clause or a what-if. Never a moral. |

## Openings

Draft one opening from each of three shapes. The type goes in `hooks[].type`.

| Type (`type`) | Shape |
|---|---|
| Scene in motion (`scene`) | In [year], [a person, by role] was [doing X] in [a cut-off place] when [the sudden failure]. |
| Paradox (`paradox`) | This is [a harmless-looking thing], and it [did something that should be impossible]. |
| Result first (`result`) | By [the next morning], [the outcome]. It began with [one small act]. |

## What holds the viewer

The viewer knows only what the person in the scene knows. One or two 'but' turns in every thirty seconds. One line that withholds, a beat before the reveal: they could not believe what they saw.

## Ending

A reveal of a few words, an ironic last clause or a what-if. The script stops on the payoff detail and asks nothing.

## Pitfalls

- A name and a date before any reason to care
- The outcome in the title or the first line (unless the opening is the result-first shape)
- No obstacle: every step succeeds
- A moral

## Template

The shape of the whole script with the facts left out. Fill it with this topic's facts; never keep its wording.

> [A person, by role] was [in the middle of an act]. But when [the first turn], [reaction]. [A constraint]. [The planted detail]. Now, [time marker], [it kept happening]. So [a decision]. When [they] looked, [they] could not believe it. It was [the reveal]. [A hard fact or an ironic clause].
